# Blue Quantum Emitters in Hexagonal Boron Nitride

- **Category**: Optoelectronic Properties / Quantum Emitters
- **Spectral Window**: $400\text{ nm} - 450\text{ nm}$ ($2.75\text{ eV} - 3.10\text{ eV}$)
- **Typical Linewidth**: $0.1 - 0.5\text{ nm}$ (Cryogenic), $1 - 3\text{ nm}$ (Room Temperature)

---

## 1. Overview
**Blue Quantum Emitters** in **Hexagonal Boron Nitride (hBN)** represent high-energy localized defect states emitting in the blue region of the optical spectrum. Unlike traditional visible-to-NIR emitters (e.g., 580 nm, 650 nm), blue emitters possess high transition frequencies, making them suitable for ultraviolet/blue quantum communication, GaN photonic waveguides, and coupling to high-bandgap semiconductor architectures.

---

## 2. Key Photophysical Characteristics

1. **High Room-Temperature Brightness**:  
   Count rates exceeding $10^5 - 10^6\text{ counts/s}$ under continuous-wave and pulsed optical pumping.
2. **High Quantum Light Purity**:  
   Quantified via the **Second-Order Autocorrelation Function**:
   $$g^{(2)}(0) < 0.2 \ll 0.5$$
   Demonstrating true single-photon emission with negligible multi-photon probability.
3. **High Debye-Waller Factor**:  
   Strong emission concentrated within the **Zero-Phonon Line (ZPL)** with moderate coupling to acoustic and optical **Phonon Sidebands (PSB)**.

---

## 3. Structural Hypotheses
While defect assignment remains an active research topic, the primary candidates for blue emission include:
- **Carbon Impurity Complexes**: $C_B V_N$, $C_N V_B$, and interstitial carbon trimers.
- **Intrinsic Point Vacancies**: Negatively charged boron vacancies ($V_B^-$) stabilized near grain boundaries.
- **Donor-Acceptor Pairs (DAP)**: Localized defect pairs exhibiting strong Coulombic dipole moments.
