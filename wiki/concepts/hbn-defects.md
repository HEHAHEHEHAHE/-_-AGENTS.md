# Hexagonal Boron Nitride (hBN) Point Defects

- **Category**: Materials Science / Crystal Defects
- **Host Crystal**: Layered 2D Hexagonal Boron Nitride (wide bandgap $\sim 6.0\text{ eV}$)
- **Key Defect Classes**: Vacancies, Carbon/Oxygen Impurities, Antisites

---

## 1. Material Properties of hBN
**Hexagonal Boron Nitride (hBN)** is an atomically flat, wide bandgap dielectric (~6 eV) with alternating boron and nitrogen atoms arranged in a honeycomb lattice. Its wide optical bandgap permits optically active deep-level defect states within the forbidden band, which act as isolated two-level quantum systems (**Single-Photon Emitters**).

---

## 2. Common Defect Configurations

| Defect Configuration | Composition | Typical Emission Band | Symmetry |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Boron Vacancy ($V_B^-$)** | Missing B atom, negatively charged | NIR (~800–850 nm, optically detected magnetic resonance) | $D_{3h} \rightarrow C_{2v}$ |
| **Nitrogen Vacancy ($V_N$)** | Missing N atom | Visible / Green (~550 nm) | $C_{2v}$ |
| **Carbon-Substituted Complexes ($C_B V_N$)** | Carbon atom substituting Boron adjacent to Nitrogen Vacancy | **Blue (~430–450 nm)** | $C_s$ (Strong Dipole) |

---

## 3. Structural Symmetry and Permanent Dipoles
In pristine monolayers, hBN exhibits $D_{3h}$ symmetry with zero out-of-plane permanent dipole. However, when point defects couple with:
1. Out-of-plane lattice corrugation or buckling
2. Interlayer interactions in multilayer stacks
3. Nearby surface adsorbates or dielectric boundaries

The inversion symmetry along the z-axis is broken, giving rise to non-zero **Permanent Electric Dipole Moments ($\vec{\mu}_z$)**, allowing for giant linear **Stark Effect** tuning.
