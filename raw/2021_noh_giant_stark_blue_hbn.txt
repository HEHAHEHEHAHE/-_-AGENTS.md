Title: Giant Stark Effect in Blue Quantum Emitters in Hexagonal Boron Nitride
Authors: G. Noh, D. Choi, H.-O. Kim, D.-G. Choi, C. Liu, G. Grosso, D. Englund, S. Jeon
Year: 2021
Journal: Nano Letters, 21(9), 3959-3965
DOI: 10.1021/acs.nanolett.1c00650

Raw Abstract & Notes:
Color centers in hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) have emerged as promising bright, room-temperature single-photon emitters (SPEs) across the visible to near-infrared spectrum. Recently, defect emitters in the blue spectral range (approx 430 - 450 nm) have attracted intensive interest due to their high brightness, narrow linewidths, and coupling to UV/blue plasmonic and optical cavity modes.
Here, we report an electrostatic investigation of blue quantum emitters in thin hBN flakes integrated into metal-insulator-metal (MIM) capacitors. By applying vertical electric fields up to 2.5 MV/cm, we observe a giant, linear Stark shift of the zero-phonon line (ZPL) spanning over 15 meV (approx 5.5 nm) without significant degradation in quantum emission purity (g^(2)(0) remains < 0.15).
Analysis reveals a massive permanent out-of-plane electric dipole moment of \mu \approx 1.8 \pm 0.2 Debye and an in-plane component of ~0.6 Debye, indicating strong localized symmetry breaking of the defect state. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations point to a carbon-associated interstitial-vacancy cluster (such as C_B V_N or related donor-acceptor complexes) residing near edge or surface boundaries. These findings demonstrate precise field-tunable blue quantum light sources for integrated quantum photonics.
